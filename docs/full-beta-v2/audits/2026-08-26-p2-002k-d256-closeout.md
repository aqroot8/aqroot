## 1. The one-sentence answer

**D-256's diagnosis is right and its instrument does not fit.** The west margin
is short of layer capacity exactly as ruled — and at the FBV2-P2-002F placement
the two LTC4368 pins that need the extra layer, `U18.7` and `U18.10`, **cannot
reach a via at any through-via geometry the board declares**, so the
`B.Cu → F.Cu → B.Cu` excursion D-256 authorises cannot be taken at all.

A layer change needs a via. A via needs somewhere to land. **U18 is an MSOP-10
on 0.50 mm pitch and the board's own floor is `min_via_diameter = 0.50 mm`.**

## 4. THE PLACEMENT ERROR, AND WHY IT IS IN THIS DOCUMENT

The first nine screens were run on the **authoritative** placement, with the
FBV2-P2-002F ECO **not** applied — `AQROOT_ECO_002F` was never set. That is the
wrong baseline: every verdict from 002F to 002J, D-255 and D-256 itself are
measured on the ECO placement, and §7 names it as the placement to use.

It was caught by §14: the R75 Kelvin branches came out **3.179 mm and
13.152 mm, a 9.973 mm mismatch**, against 002I's measured 4.464 / 4.464 mm and
0.000 mm. A mismatch that large is not a routing outcome, it is a different
board — and it was.

The runs were repeated on the ECO placement (§5B). **Both sets are reported
here, labelled**, because the comparison turns out to be the most decision-
relevant result in the task: the same D-256 strategy succeeds on one placement
and is physically unavailable on the other, and that is the finding.

## 6. Three harness defects found and fixed

All three are placement-independent and all five suites still PASS with them in.

### PR-45 — the via site must be REACHABLE, not merely nearby

`connect_hop` chose its via site with `nearest_free()`, which answers *where is
the closest point a via fits* — a question about geometry. The question that
matters is *where is the closest point a via fits **that this pad can get to***.
Beside a fine-pitch pin the two are not the same place: for `U18.10`,
`nearest_free()` returned a site 2.30 mm away on the far side of the copper the
pin was trying to escape past, the walk to it failed, and the hop was abandoned.

`QBoard.via_site()` now floods the near layer at track width from the escape
point and takes the nearest cell of that reachable region which also admits the
via on both layers. A site returned that way is reachable by construction. It
also rejects sites that would violate `min_hole_to_hole` — a drill-to-drill
rule the copper-clearance grids cannot see, and the reason the first
`U18.10 → Q3.4` escape came back `hole_to_hole: 1`.

### PR-46 — say why, and say that it happened at all

Two silences, both material:

1. `connect_hop` reported **every** failure as `NO_PATH: no F corridor`,
   including the two that never reach the far layer — no reachable via site, and
   no near-layer walk to it. The far-layer run is the last thing it tries, so a
   hop that died before ever attempting it was still being reported as an F.Cu
   **capacity** failure. That misreport is what made the layer option look
   exhausted when it had not been exercised. It now returns `NO_VIA_SITE` and
   `NO_NEAR_WALK`, and those exact strings are what §5B's control run reports.
2. A connection **rejected by the DRC / ratsnest gate was reverted and requeued
   in complete silence** — no log line, no journal entry. Reading the first
   screen, three items simply were not in the transcript. "Not attempted" and
   "routed, judged, and thrown away by DRC" are not the same finding. The gate
   rejection now prints, with the DRC class and the rule text — which is how
   `BAT_MAIN routed clearance 0.3000 mm; actual 0.2500 mm` became visible at all.

### D-249 corridor areas now span both outer layers

The named rule areas were created on B.Cu alone, which was true while every
battery route was on B.Cu. The moment a D-249-ruled tap takes an F.Cu escape,
`A.enclosedByArea('BAT_RAW_TAP_U18')` is false for the F.Cu half of its own
corridor, the 0.20 mm ruling silently stops applying, and the segments are
judged against BAT_RAW's 0.60 mm class floor — `{"track_width": 5}` on
`U18.1 → R77.1`. These areas restrict nothing (`DoNotAllow*` are all False);
they only *name* a region so a width rule can be conditioned on it. Extending
the name to the other outer layer of the same corridor weakens nothing and makes
the existing ruling apply where the copper actually is.

## 9. New CTO issues

**D-257 (requested) — D-256's instrument does not fit U18's pin field.**
At the FBV2-P2-002F placement, `U18.7` has no legal escape at any approved width
and `U18.10` has no reachable through-via site at any diameter from 0.60 mm down
to 0.25 mm. A 0.50 mm-pitch MSOP-10 does not admit the board's own 0.50 mm
minimum through via. The options are all fabrication or placement rulings, not
routing ones:

  (a) **microvia / via-in-pad** — the project already declares
      `min_microvia_diameter 0.20`, `min_microvia_drill 0.10`, and a reachable
      0.20 mm site exists 1.20 mm out from `U18.10`. This is a stackup, cost and
      vendor-capability decision;
  (b) **change U18's land pattern** (pad extension / fan-out), a footprint and
      assembly decision;
  (c) **move U18** — forbidden by §7, and 002J exhausted the neighbouring lever;
  (d) **accept `U18.7` and `U18.10` unrouted** and re-scope the LTC4368 trip
      network accordingly.

**PR-47 — the Q3 south row is a proved land-pattern conflict.** `Q3_CS` and
`LTC_GATE` interleave across four adjacent SOIC-8 pads with one B.Cu slot;
whichever routes second loses its middle pad's escape entirely, on both layers.
It belongs in `router_regression.py`'s pinned-conflict table beside U11.2,
U14.2/U14.3 and U18.8/U18.9, and it needs a ruling: Q3 rotation, a gate/CS pad
reassignment, or acceptance.

**PR-48 — D-249 relaxes width, not clearance.** `U18.1`'s ruled 0.20 mm VIN tap
is rejected by `BAT_MAIN routed clearance` (0.300 mm required, 0.250 mm actual)
against PR-43's own bridge copper at `R77.1`. The same rule blocks the U14.2 /
U14.3 gauge branches at 0.2347 / 0.2350 mm. Either the corridor areas must carry
a clearance constraint as well as a width one, or these taps need a different
topology.

**§6 flag — LTC_OV did not stay on B.Cu.** It reached F.Cu through the generic
fallback rather than any D-256 decision, and it is a high-impedance comparator
input. §6 says not to move it silently; this is the not-silent part.
