## 1. The three candidate causes, discriminated by measurement

### (A) intrinsic east-row pad / footprint / board-edge geometry — **REFUTED**

On the empty authoritative board (0 signal tracks; U19 is **not** moved by the c3
placement — c3_00 moves only R75/U18/R79) the escape count at ≥ 0.150 mm is:

| pad | position | ways out (empty board) |
|---|---|---|
| `VREC_VCC U19.8` | (3.833, 29.230), NE corner, east row | **5** |
| `VBRIDGE_TOP R85.1` | (8.000, 28.725) | **8** |
| `VBRIDGE_TOP D10.1` | (1.195, 6.265) | **7** |

All three leave their own copper freely — intrinsic geometry is not the blocker.

### (B) route-order / already-laid copper — **CONFIRMED (the cause)**

**The contention is local to the dead-cell block.** U19 (y ≈ 29) is
geographically isolated from the western-margin trunk congestion (y 60–93). A
cheap **dead-cell-only** real-router reproduction (`AQROOT_LOCAL=DEADCELL`, a new
bounded probe prefix in the class of R80/D256/U19 that routes only stages 10a/10b
and skips the whole west-margin prefix) reproduces the blocker byte-similar:
`U19.8 NO LEGAL ESCAPE … blocked by track (×34), U19.7 (×14), board_edge (×5)`
against the full run's `track (×33), U19.7 (×13), board_edge (×5), U19.5 (×5)`.
So the west-margin prefix contributes **nothing** to U19.8's blockers; the
33–34 blocking `track` hits are all **dead-cell copper**.

**The aggressor is the D-277 crossing pin `REF_POL U19.2`.** Isolated on the
board, the driver's order routes `N_POL TP23.1→U19.3` (6.118 mm, B.Cu) then
`REF_POL TP24.1→U19.2`, and only then U19.8. Obstacle attribution on the real
routed board (`U19.8` escape rays vs each laid segment, tagged by the connection
that laid it):

- `REF_POL TP24.1→U19.2` routes **13.372 mm** for a ~7.5 mm connection — a
  northern **horseshoe**. Its segments seal U19.8:
  - `(4.65, 29.85)→(3.05, 29.85)` — a horizontal wall at y = 29.85 spanning
    x 3.05–4.65, **d = 0.495 mm** directly north of U19.8 (x = 3.833),
  - `(6.90, 27.60)→(4.65, 29.85)` — a diagonal, **d = 0.901 mm**, sealing the east.
- Why the horseshoe: D-277 correctly routes U19.3 first; U19.3's copper (running
  east–west at y ≈ 25.95) then fills U19.2's **direct southern lane** to
  TP24.1 (y = 22.5), so U19.2 — a west-row single-lane pin — is forced to detour
  **north**, straight over the east-row corner pin U19.8.
- After the wall, U19.8 has **0 B.Cu escape** — so it cannot even start the
  ordinary F.Cu-hop fallback (a hop needs a short B.Cu escape at the pad). That
  is why it fails hard rather than hopping.

**The D-277 planar tie-break cannot catch this.** D-277 compares a pin's
straight-line pad→target *span* to a tied sibling's pad. U19.2's span
(x 1.56–6, y 22.5–28.58) does **not** contain U19.8 (y = 29.23); the *route*
detours outside the span because of laid copper. The proxy under-predicts the
crossing, and U19.8 is a MULTI-LANE victim (5 ways), not a member of the tied
single-lane class — so no cardinality-0 span tie-break applies here (this is the
different-class warning D-277 raised, now confirmed).

### (C) minimum placement ECO — **NOT REQUIRED**

(B) is fixable without moving any part (the layer hop, §2), so cause (C) is left
un-exercised per the A→B→C investigation order.

## 2. The fix — a layer hop for the boxed crossing pin (cardinality-0 placement)

`order_tight` already computes, for the boxed single-lane tied class, a
`crossings[i]` term (the D-277 predicate, guarded by `fr_a ≤ 1`). 003F reuses
that exact predicate: when `crossings[i] > 0` the pin is the one whose B.Cu route
will detour antisocially, so it is recorded in `hop_first_keys`, and `run_once`
tries an **ordinary 0.35/0.20 through-via F.Cu hop first** for those keys (the
D-257 preferred via, no rule relaxed), falling through to the ordinary B.Cu
ladder untouched on failure.

Measured: `REF_POL TP24.1→U19.2` then routes **8.610 mm on F.Cu with 2 ordinary
vias, direct** (no horseshoe). With no B.Cu wall laid, `U19.8` returns to **5
ways** and **routes** (`U19.8→R84.2` 22.408 mm), and `R85.1` stays escapable
through the U19.8 fix.

**Scope / blast radius.** The mark fires **only** in the D-277 crossing class
(`crossings > 0`, `fr_a ≤ 1`) — currently only `REF_POL U19.2` — so it is inert
for every pin with a second way out or no crossing, exactly like the D-277
tie-break it shares its predicate with. It changes only which *layer* the
already-identified crossing pin runs on; it adds an option to `run_once` and
removes none; the hop uses ordinary through-vias at the board's declared
geometry, no width/clearance/netclass rule is relaxed, and no authoritative
copper is laid (base config).

## 4. The co-terminal `VBRIDGE_TOP R85.1` is a DISTINCT blocker → 003G

`R85.1` was **never routed in 003E either** (it is not in `phaseA_003e_fix.json`);
it is a co-terminal, not a consequence of U19.8. On the real routed 003F board
its escape is sealed by a **different aggressor**: obstacle attribution counts,
within 2.0 mm of R85.1, **N_POL ×10** and BAT_RAW ×3 — the N_POL chain leaving
R85.2 (R85's own other pad) and the 31.19 mm `N_POL R86.1→TP23.1` detour, **not**
the REF_POL crossing that boxes U19.8. Neither R85.1 (8 ways) nor its N_POL
aggressor R86.1 (8 ways) is single-lane on the empty board, so the D-278
predicate correctly does **not** mark them: the fix is scoped and does not — and
must not — claim to close R85.1.

The same pattern holds one sub-cluster over: `REF_HO R92.1↔R93.2` is boxed by
`REC_GATE_N ×8`. These are the **dead-cell resistor-field congestion** class — a
packed 0402 cluster (R84–R96 at 0.65 mm pitch around U19 and the recovery FETs
Q5–Q9) where multiple nets' escapes box each other and the casualty rotates. It
is a genuinely different problem from the single U19.8 crossing, needs its own
bounded study, and is defined as **FBV2-P2-003G** below. (The BPP closure
`D9.1/C25.1/C36.1/C58.1 NO_LEGAL_ESCAPE at ≥ 1.20 mm` is the KNOWN blocker the
