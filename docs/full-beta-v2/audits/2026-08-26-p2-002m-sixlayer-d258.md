## 3. The six-layer migration (§1–§5)

`checks/sixlayer.py` performs and then **re-measures** the migration:

```
copper layer count            6
layer names / order           F.Cu In1.Cu In2.Cu In3.Cu In4.Cu B.Cu
explicit stackup present      yes
stack thickness               1.6028 mm
In1.Cu GND plane              1 zone, 1 island, net GND
In4.Cu GND plane              1 zone, 1 island, net GND
outer / inner-signal pours    0 / 0
signal tracks / vias          0 / 0
board outline                 72.100 x 148.100 mm
```

`In4.Cu` is built from **the In1 pour's own outline**, so the two references
cover exactly the same area and neither can drift from the other. The In1 guard
was **extended, not weakened**: `In4.Cu carries GND only` is the identical rule,
and `SWITCH_NODE: outer layers only` is repeated for In3 so the second new
signal layer inherits the first one's protection.

**The six-layer DRC histogram equals the four-layer baseline exactly** —
`{hole_clearance: 5, lib_footprint_issues: 199, solder_mask_bridge: 1,
unconnected_items: 499}`. The migration itself introduces no violation class.

Stackup geometry, and an honest limit on it: the **outer 0.2104 mm 7628
prepreg** is the one figure carried from JLC04161H-7628. The inner distribution
(0.2 core / 0.6312 prepreg / 0.2 core) is **DERIVED** to total 1.6 mm at 1 oz
outer and 0.5 oz inner and is recorded as a derivation. **It must be confirmed
against JLCPCB's published table before Gerbers are ordered**, and it is not
quoted as manufacturer data anywhere in this repository.

## 4. Runtime four-layer assumptions found and fixed (§4)

The router had `LNAME = {'F': F_Cu, 'B': B_Cu}` and eleven `for L in ('F','B')`
loops. That was *true* while the board was four layers with In1 a reference and
In2 reserved for slow power — and it is exactly the assumption §4 asks to be
found. Three defects followed from it, and each was caught by DRC on real
copper rather than by reading:

1. **The obstacle model saw two layers.** `QBoard` now derives its copper set
   and its routable set from the board (`ROUTABLE = {4: (F,B), 6: (F,B,I2,I3)}`),
   and `via()` registers a through via's copper on **every** layer, not two.
2. **`connect_hop` sited its via by checking only `near` and `far`.** Harmless
   while those *were* the stack; on six layers it dropped a via straight onto
   another net's inner copper and DRC answered `shorting_items`. Both hop and
   POFV siting now require the site clear on **all six layers**.
3. **`connect_hop` could only ever reach F.Cu.** The new internal signal layers
   were unreachable, so the capacity D-258 buys could not be spent. `far` now
   defaults to every routable layer that is not `near` — **except for wide
   nets**, which are held to the outer layers because §2 says so and because
   `BAT_MAIN is outer-layer only` already said so. Offering In2/In3 to
   `BAT_SENSE` produced that rule three times on one connection.

`add_named_area` now spans every copper layer the board has, deriving the set
from the board rather than listing it — the same defect FBV2-P2-002K found at
four layers, which six layers makes available on four more.

## 5. PR-47 — Q3 POFV: CLOSED on scratch (§6, §7)

```
Q3   Package_SO:SOIC-8_3.9x4.9mm_P1.27mm
     pitch 1.270 mm, pads 1.950 x 0.600 mm, copper gap 0.670 mm
     south row: 1 Q3_CS  2 LTC_GATE  3 Q3_CS  4 LTC_GATE

Q3_CS  Q3.3 -> Q3.1   4.626 mm at 0.25 mm on In2.Cu
                      via-in-pad 0.35 / 0.20 at Q3.3 (3.165, 59.275)
                      ordinary via 0.35 / 0.20 at (5.700, 57.800)
                      pad copper remaining 0.125 mm each side
LTC_GATE Q3.2 -> Q3.4  5.500 mm on B.Cu, ZERO vias
```

`Q3_CS` is **one component**. `LTC_GATE` is **one component** across all six
functional pads. The premium process is spent on **one pad**; `Q3.1` keeps an
ordinary external via because it has four escape directions and does not need
one. No footprint was modified, no toe was extended, and no via-in-pad was
added to any other pad.

### Scheduling, and why it had to change

The POFV is a **through** via, so its site must be clear on all six layers
including F.Cu. Scheduled after the BAT_MAIN chain it was not: `BAT_SENSE
Q3.6 → R75.1` had taken an F.Cu hop whose 1.00 mm track runs down x = 2.800
past Q3's whole row, **0.365 mm from `Q3.3`'s centre**, and the POFV returned
`POFV_LAYER_CONFLICT on F`. `Q3.3` has exactly one point in this design; a
1.00 mm trunk has the whole board. The escape is now scheduled **first**, by the
same scarcity argument PR-18 and PR-36 already won.

The fabrication note is in
[`architecture/FBV2_SIXLAYER_STACKUP.md`](../architecture/FBV2_SIXLAYER_STACKUP.md):
**PLATED OVER FILLED VIA — resin-filled, capped, plated, planarised.** Not
tented, not mask-plugged, not open, not blind, not a microvia. Gerbers alone do
not force a fabricator to select it, which is why it is written down as a
process order rather than left implied by geometry.

Two blockers, both caused by the POFV taking the space it needs:

- **`BAT_SENSE Q3.6 → R75.1`** is rejected by `BAT_MAIN routed clearance
  0.3000 mm; actual 0.2400 mm`. The 1.00 mm trunk and the POFV copper want the
  same corridor, and a wide net cannot be moved to an inner layer to resolve it
  because §2 keeps high-current copper on outer 1 oz. The Kelvin branch itself
  is intact — `R75.1 → U18.9` is 3.179 mm — but the net is not one component,
  so the Kelvin criterion cannot be reported as met.
- **`LTC_OV R77.2 → R78.1`** returns `NO_VIA_SITE`. §13 requires LTC_OV to stay
  local and preferably B.Cu, and forbids the long generic F.Cu fallback as a
  final design; with the all-layer via check in force it now finds no legal site
  at all. **This is the §13 stop condition in substance**: LTC_OV is not merely
  taking a long excursion, it is not routing.

**The six extra layers did not remove this.** They removed the *Q3* conflict,
which is what they were bought for, and the west margin's remaining shortage is
a clearance-and-corridor contest between a 1.00 mm trunk and a fine-pitch escape
that both need the same few square millimetres. That is a placement question,
and the U18/R75 levers §10–§12 authorise are the ones that address it.

## 7. Opportunity and simplification scan (§15)

- **The premium process is confined to one pad.** Q3.3 alone; everything else
  is an ordinary through via. This is the "prefer standard fabrication" outcome
  §15 asks for, and it survived the migration.
- **A long outer detour was replaced by a short inner route**: `Q3_CS` went from
  *unroutable* to 4.626 mm on In2.Cu, and the gate drive it was fighting went
  from a 15.991 mm F.Cu excursion (002L's only option) to **5.500 mm on B.Cu
  with zero vias**. That is the clearest single win of the migration.
- **Sensitive nets stay on outer layers against a plane.** Nothing
  impedance-sensitive was moved inward; both new signal layers sit between two
  solid references and are reserved for control routing.
- **High-current copper stayed on outer 1 oz**, now enforced in the router
  rather than only in the rules.
- **Via count did not balloon**: 22 vias on the whole local prefix, of which 8
  are the D-257/POFV 0.35/0.20 fine vias and the rest are ordinary.
- **No test point was found blocking a functional escape.**
- **A routing-only exception was retired**: the D-257 escape corridors no longer
  carry a clearance floor — see §8.

## 8. The rule correction, a second time

The D-257 fine-escape corridors were emitting a `clearance (min 0.20mm)` floor
alongside their via-geometry override. The corridor is grown from the laid track
with a 0.3 mm tolerance, so it swallows neighbouring copper, and the floor then
fires on pairs it was never meant to govern:
`LTC_OV R77.2 → R78.1` was rejected by
`'D-257 LTC_GATE U18.10->Q3.4 escape ... clearance 0.2000 mm; actual 0.1000 mm'`.

**This is FBV2-P2-002L's lesson repeating: a relaxation applied where nothing
needed relaxing is a restriction.** The escape corridors now carry **via
geometry only**. The three measured PR-48 cases keep their clearance in
`FIXED_CLR`, where they were measured, and §9's instruction not to expand their
scope is honoured.

**PR-48 re-verified on the six-layer scratch:** `U18.1` VIN connected in the
BAT_RAW main island; `U14.2`, `U14.3` and `TP15.1` all in the
`BAT_PROTECTED_P` trunk island.
