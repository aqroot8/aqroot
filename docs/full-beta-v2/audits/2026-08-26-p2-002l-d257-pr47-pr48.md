## 2. Process fix — a run now states which board it is on

002K ran nine screens on the wrong placement. It was caught by a downstream
Kelvin anomaly four hours in, and it could as easily have been caught after an
authoritative write. The failure was not a forgotten flag; it was that
**nothing in a run stated which board it was routing.**

`checks/placement_fingerprint.py` prints the pose of U18, R75, R76–R83, Q2, Q3,
U14 and U19 at the head of every screen, and `AQROOT_EXPECT_PLACEMENT` turns
that into an assertion — `AUTHORITATIVE`, `ECO_002F`, or a candidate JSON. A
mismatch is `PLACEMENT IDENTITY MISMATCH — refusing to route`, **before any
copper**, naming every part that differs and both values.

The candidate loader reads the **same file** the assertion checks, so what is
applied and what is verified cannot drift apart. Every screen in this task ran
under it; all eleven printed `PLACEMENT ASSERTED`.

It paid for itself immediately: candidate C02 of the first search came back
with `courtyards_overlap: 3` on every connection, and the fingerprint made it
obvious that the geometric filter had been measuring courtyards against a board
the screen would never see — see §5.

## 3. PR-48 — local fine-pitch clearance: PROVEN

D-249 ruled **width** by path role and said nothing about **clearance**. The two
are not interchangeable, and three ruled microamp branches were unroutable for a
reason nobody had ruled.

The rule is a `clearance (min 0.20mm)` constraint conditioned on
`A.NetName == '<net>' && A.enclosedByArea('<corridor>')`, written last in the
generated `.kicad_dru` so it wins, reaching one net inside one bounded corridor.
It does not touch global board clearance, trunk spacing, edge clearance or any
hole rule.

| case | before (002K) | after |
|---|---|---|
| **A · `U18.1` VIN** | `BAT_MAIN routed clearance 0.3000 mm; actual 0.2500 mm` | **routes — 10.107 mm at 0.20 mm**, F.Cu, 2 vias |
| **B · `U14.2` branch** | `clearance 0.3000 mm; actual 0.2347 mm` | **routes — 7.401 mm at 0.15 mm**, B.Cu, 0 vias |
| **C · `U14.3` branch** | `clearance 0.3000 mm; actual 0.2350 mm` | **joined via U14.2**, one island with the trunk |

`BAT_PROTECTED_P` afterwards: `C25.1, D9.1, R75.2, TP15.1, U11.2, U14.2, U14.3,
U18.8` — **one island**. Minimum measured clearance in the relaxed corridors is
**0.200 mm**; no new DRC class appeared.

### The correction that had to be made

The first cut of this list also covered `BAT_PROT_TAP_U18` and
`BAT_SENSE_KELVIN`, on the reasoning that they share U18's pin field. **That was
wrong and the board said so within one screen.** A clearance rule states a
*minimum*; this block is written last so it wins; and both corridors were
already running legally at **0.150 mm** under the pad-escape rules. Adding a
0.20 mm floor there relaxed nothing — it **raised** the requirement on compliant
copper, and `BAT_SENSE Kelvin sense tap … clearance 0.2000 mm; actual
0.1500 mm` then rejected every connection after it.

**A relaxation applied where nothing needed relaxing is a restriction.** §4 says
to use the smallest corridor necessary; the list is now exactly the corridors
that were measured to need it.

## 4. D-257 — the ordinary through via: PROVEN, and the reserve was not needed

D-257 rules **0.35 / 0.20 preferred**, **0.25 / 0.15 absolute reserve**, nothing
smaller, and no blind, buried or laser microvia. 002K's finding that a 0.20 mm
site exists for `U18.10` is therefore **not a route** — `min_microvia_diameter`
is a CAD default, not a fabricator's authorisation.

Both ruled geometries sit under the board's global `min_via_diameter` 0.50 mm
and `min_via_annular_width` 0.125 mm, so each bounded escape corridor carries
its own `via_diameter` / `annular_width` / `hole_size` override. **Measured
before adopting it:** a 0.35/0.20 through via inside a named area reports
`via_diameter` and `annular_width` without the rules and **nothing at all** with
them; no other violation class moves either way.

Every via actually laid on the U18 8/8 board:

| geometry | net | count | how |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0.35 / 0.20** | LTC_GATE | 4 | **planned D-256 escape, D-257 preferred** |
| **0.35 / 0.20** | LTC_SHDN | 2 | **planned D-256 escape, D-257 preferred** |
| 0.60 / 0.30 | LTC_OV, LTC_UV, LTC_SHDN | 6 | ordinary Default-class fallback |
| 0.80 / 0.40 | BAT_RAW, BAT_SENSE | 4 | ordinary wide-net fallback |

**The 0.25/0.15 reserve was never taken.** Every D-256 layer excursion this task
needed is carried by the preferred geometry, with two layer transitions per
connection and no more.

## 5. The U18 search — and why it does not close

§6 lifted the prohibition on moving U18. The search is a **rigid group** — U18
plus its own divider and trip ring R76..R83 — translated ±9 mm at 0.5 mm and
rotated 0/90/180/270 about the group centroid, seeded from **both** families,
with R75, Q2, Q3, U14 and the FET chain fixed. Filters: courtyard/edge/rule-area
legality, an outward band on all eight signal pins, Kelvin ≤ 10 mm each and
≤ 5 mm mismatch, then a bare-board escape **and reachable 0.35/0.20 via site**
for `U18.7` and `U18.10` — the two pins 002K proved are binding.

Five poses survived the analytic filters; four survived stage E; **four serious
candidates** were screened on the real local prefix, plus the unshifted
authoritative pose.

| pose | Kelvin (straight-line) | screened U18 | open pins |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AUTHORITATIVE** `3.000,72.400/90` | 2.440 / 8.265 · **5.825 ✗** | **8/8** | none |
| C00 = **002F ECO** `8.000,65.250/180` | 4.464 / 4.464 · **0.000 ✓** | 6/8 | `U18.7`, `U18.10` |
| C01 `8.500,67.400/90` | 6.748 / 6.568 · 0.180 ✓ | 5/8 | `U18.2`, `U18.3`, `U18.10` |
| C02 `9.000,67.400/90` | 7.210 / 7.006 · 0.204 ✓ | 5/8 | `U18.2`, `U18.3`, `U18.10` |
| C03 `8.500,63.900/90` | 8.742 / 5.705 · 3.037 ✓ | **7/8** | `U18.10` |

**No pose satisfies §8 simultaneously.** One routes all eight pins and cannot
measure the shunt symmetrically; every pose that measures the shunt loses pins.

### Two things that sharpen this, and both matter

**Bare-board stage E is negative evidence only, again.** C00 — the plain 002F
ECO — passed stage E: on a *bare* board `U18.7` and `U18.10` both reach a
0.35/0.20 site. On the real prefix it reproduced 002K exactly: `U18.7`
`NO_LEGAL_ESCAPE`, `U18.10` `NO_VIA_SITE`. PR-40 has now been confirmed a third
time; stage E is a *necessary* condition and never a sufficient one.

**Most of the authoritative pose's Kelvin failure is ROUTING, not placement.**
Straight-line it is 2.440 / 8.265, mismatch **5.825 mm** — over §8's 5 mm limit
by **0.825 mm**. As routed it came out 3.179 / 13.152, mismatch **9.973 mm**.
So **4.948 mm of the mismatch is a detour on `R75.2 → U18.8`**, not geometry.
The pose is one short branch away from the limit, and the branch is being sent
the long way round.

**The lever §6 held back is R75.** §6 says to keep R75 fixed *initially*, and
the Kelvin constraint is a relationship between `U18.8`/`U18.9` and
`R75.1`/`R75.2`. Moving the sense resistor toward U18 addresses the 0.825 mm
directly without disturbing the pin escapes that only the authoritative pose
achieves. It was not taken here because R75 sits in the 1.5 A path and that is a
larger ruling than this task carries.

## 6. PR-47 — Q3: ordinary vias cannot solve it

**Measured Q3 land pattern** (`Package_SO:SOIC-8_3.9x4.9mm_P1.27mm`, B.Cu,
`3.800, 56.800` rot 90):

```
pitch                      1.270 mm
pad size                   1.950 x 0.600 mm
copper gap, adjacent pads  0.670 mm
south row   pin 1 Q3_CS  pin 2 LTC_GATE  pin 3 Q3_CS  pin 4 LTC_GATE
```

Two nets interleave across four adjacent pads with one B.Cu slot. On the U18
8/8 board, with Q3_CS having lost the slot:

```
Q3.1  Q3_CS     escape 0.25 mm, 4 dirs   via 0.35/0.20 at 1.33 mm out
Q3.2  LTC_GATE  escape 0.25 mm, 1 dir    via 0.35/0.20 at 1.45 mm out
Q3.3  Q3_CS     NO LEGAL ESCAPE at 0.25, 0.20 AND 0.15 mm
                blocked by Q3.2 (x27), Q3.4 (x20) and track
Q3.4  LTC_GATE  escape 0.25 mm, 4 dirs   via 0.35/0.20 at 1.45 mm out
```

Every ordinary-via attempt on `Q3.1 → Q3.3`, both D-257 geometries and all three
widths, returns `NO_LEGAL_ESCAPE`:

```
via 0.35/0.20  w = 0.25 / 0.20 / 0.15   NO_LEGAL_ESCAPE
via 0.25/0.15  w = 0.25 / 0.20 / 0.15   NO_LEGAL_ESCAPE
B.Cu           w = 0.25 / 0.20 / 0.15   NO_LEGAL_ESCAPE
```

**The reason is structural, not marginal.** A layer excursion needs a via; a via
needs a landing site; a landing site has to be *reached* from the pad. `Q3.3`
cannot emit legal copper in any direction at any legal width, so there is no
escape to reach a via from — and the via's own size is irrelevant. This is the
same shape as `U18.7` at the 002F pose, one package over.

Placement, footprint and via-in-pad were all left untouched first, exactly as
§12 requires. **§13's gate is therefore reached.**

### §13 — via-in-pad, geometry only

A **filled and capped ordinary THROUGH via inside the pad**, measured against
the pad geometry above:

| | 0.35 / 0.20 preferred | 0.25 / 0.15 reserve |
|---|---|---|
| via land inside a 0.600 mm pad width | 0.35 mm — **fits**, **0.125 mm** of pad copper each side | 0.25 mm — fits, 0.175 mm each side |
| along the 1.950 mm pad length | ample | ample |
| pad-to-pad copper gap | **unchanged at 0.670 mm** — the via is inside existing pad copper and creates no new copper-to-copper spacing | unchanged |
| drill-to-drill, pads 1 and 3 | 2.540 mm vs 0.25 mm minimum | 2.540 mm |

**Geometrically feasible at the preferred D-257 geometry.** Nothing was applied
to the authoritative PCB, no footprint was modified, and no blind or microvia is
involved.

**This is a fabrication-architecture decision, and it is the stop.** Filled +
capped + planarised via-in-pad is a premium process. §13 says to report it
explicitly because it triggers the next decision: **4-layer with premium
filled/capped via-in-pad, versus a 6-layer stackup.**

### §14 — the land-pattern alternative, recorded and not taken

Extending the pad toes outward would give `Q3.3` an escape without via-in-pad.
The required change is a **toe extension on Q3 pins 1–4 of ≥ 0.40 mm** (enough
to put a 0.25 mm track plus 0.20 mm clearance beyond the current pad end, clear
of the neighbouring pads' 0.670 mm gap). **Not applied.** A land-pattern change
needs its own datasheet and manufacturing ruling.

## 7. §15 — six-layer impact assessment (non-destructive, triggered)

Triggered because the 4-layer design now requires via-in-pad for PR-47. **No
authoritative 6-layer change was made.**

- **Proposed layer roles.** `F.Cu` signal / `In1.Cu` GND / `In2.Cu` signal +
  power pours / `In3.Cu` GND / `In4.Cu` signal / `B.Cu` signal. Two extra
  routing layers with a second solid GND reference, so every control escape has
  a reference plane on both sides.
- **Thickness.** 1.6 mm is retainable — 6-layer 1.6 mm is a stock offering; core
  and prepreg thicknesses shrink, dielectric per layer drops. **P1's mechanical
  evidence depends on outline, hole positions, component heights and the 1.6 mm
  overall thickness, all unchanged**, so the enclosure fit, the M2 retention
  points and the J5 mating-face clearance stay valid as measured.
- **Files affected.** `.kicad_pcb` layer stack, `.kicad_pro` design settings
  (layer count, per-layer netclass assignment), `.kicad_dru` (In1-only GND
  language in the current rules names one plane and would need to name two), and
  the harness's `wide_nets` / layer constants.
- **Routing benefit for U18 and Q3.** **Direct for U18** — a second signal layer
  reachable from the same escapes relieves the west margin without any new
  via geometry. **Indirect for Q3, and it does not remove the root cause**:
  `Q3.3` still has no escape on B.Cu, so it still needs either a via inside the
  pad or a land-pattern change. **A 6-layer stackup does not by itself solve
  PR-47.**
- **Effect on later routing.** Substantially easier MCU, display and expansion
  routing; the RF/NFC blocks gain a second reference plane, which is a genuine
  improvement for the 433/915 and NFC arms.
- **Revalidation required.** Impedance-sensitive nets (USB, NFC transmit arms,
  the display SPI) would need their stackup-dependent rules re-derived; the
  In1-GND-continuity guards in `p1_regression` and `dru_probe` would need to
  cover both planes; DRC/ERC and all five suites re-run.
- **Cost note.** 6-layer is a standard process; filled/capped via-in-pad on
  4-layer is a premium one. **The two options are not obviously ordered on cost,
  and the decision is the CTO's.**

- **A smaller ordinary via avoided a premium process for U18** — the whole D-256
  escape set is carried by 0.35/0.20 ordinary through vias. This is exactly the
  "prefer standard fabrication" outcome §18 asks for, and it is why the U18 half
  of D-257 closes.
- **The same local 0.20 mm clearance rule cleanly solves U18.1 *and* U14.2/U14.3**
  — one ruling, three cases, two corridors, no other net touched.
- **The reserve via was not needed anywhere.** Recorded so it stays in reserve.
- **No test point was found blocking a functional escape.** TP15 rides the U14
  corridor and joins the trunk island; TP17/TP18/TP19/TP20 are §13 items and were
  not routed.
- **No passive relocation removes a via** in the measured configuration: the
  four LTC_GATE and two LTC_SHDN vias are pin-field escapes, not detours around
  a passive.
