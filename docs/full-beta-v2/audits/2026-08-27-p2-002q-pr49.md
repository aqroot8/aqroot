## 3. §3–§5 — PR-49 as a general router ruling

`run()` treated a rung as accepted the moment `connect_role` returned
geometrically ok, and the DRC/connectivity gate ran afterwards. A rung that
routed and was then rejected abandoned the whole connection, so the remaining
rungs of an **already authorised** ladder were never tried.

The rule now lives in one place, `route_battery_block.ladder_retry`, so it can
be regression-tested directly rather than by reading:

```
attempt(lad) -> (True,  None)   routed AND passed every gate
                (False, w)      rung `w` routed, gate rejected it, copper reverted
                (False, None)   failed for a reason that is not a gate rejection
```

On a gate rejection the ladder is truncated to the rungs **strictly narrower**
than the rejected one and the attempt repeats. On any other failure it stops
immediately.

**The safety boundary is structural, not a promise.** The retry only ever walks
the ladder the path role already had, so it cannot invent a rung, cannot go
below a standing floor, touches no netclass and no clearance, never suppresses
DRC, and never keeps copper from a rejected rung. For `PLAN_1_BPP_TRUNK` the
authorised ladder remains exactly **1.50 mm target / 1.20 mm floor**.

### §5 — the standing regression, G9 in `router_regression.py`

| check | result |
|---|---|
| gate-rejected rung falls to the next authorised rung | **PASS** — tried `[1.5, 1.2]` then `[1.2]`, accepted |
| the retry never invents a rung below the ladder | **PASS** — rungs offered are exactly the ladder |
| every rung rejected leaves the connection failed | **PASS** — 2 attempts, then failure |
| a non-gate failure does not walk the ladder | **PASS** — 1 attempt, stops |
| a rejected rung leaves no copper on the board | **PASS** — 0 → 5 → 0 tracks, `laid` 0 → 0 |

`router_regression`: **ALL CHECKS PASS**, G1–G9.

same-net argument in §9 — that a tap sharing its net with the trunk cannot

that `LTC_UV`, `LTC_OV`, `LTC_SHDN` and `FAULT_N` need. The hypothesis is
therefore **rejected on evidence**, and the flag (`AQROOT_KELVIN_FIRST`) is left
in the harness, off, so the measurement is reproducible.

## 7. The finding worth carrying forward

**Closing the trunk and holding U18 at 8/8 are in direct contention on this
placement.** 002P reported 8/8 with `R75.2 → D9.1` unrouted; PR-49 lays that
trunk and three control pins fall. The two results are not in conflict — they
are the same board measured with one more connection on it.

That is a new blocker of a kind none of 002M–002P has faced: not a corridor, not
a via, not a clearance rule, but **a contention between the high-current trunk
and the LTC4368 pin field for the same lanes**, exposed only once the trunk could
actually be laid. It is a routing-order and corridor problem in the same family
as the Kelvin detour, and both now sit on the same board.
