## The answer

**D-256's diagnosis is right and its instrument does not fit.** The west margin
is short of layer capacity exactly as ruled. But at the FBV2-P2-002F placement
the two pins that need the extra layer — `U18.7` and `U18.10` — **cannot reach a
via at any through-via geometry the board declares**, so the B.Cu → F.Cu → B.Cu
excursion D-256 authorises cannot be taken at all.

A layer change needs a via. A via needs somewhere to land. U18 is an MSOP-10 on
**0.50 mm pitch** and the board's floor is **`min_via_diameter = 0.50 mm`**.

```
U18.10   escape 0.20 mm, ONE direction
         reachable via site at 0.60 / 0.55 / 0.50 / 0.45 / 0.40 / 0.35 /
                               0.30 / 0.25 mm .......... NONE
         reachable via site at 0.20 mm ................. yes, 1.20 mm out
                                        (= min_microvia_diameter, NOT a through via)

U18.7    NO LEGAL ESCAPE at 0.25, 0.20 AND 0.15 mm
         blocked by U18.8 and U18.6 - its own adjacent pads
```

## The placement error

The first nine screens ran without `AQROOT_ECO_002F`, i.e. on the authoritative
placement rather than the FBV2-P2-002F ECO that §7 names and that every
002F–002J verdict uses. It was caught by §14: the R75 Kelvin branches came out
**3.179 / 13.152 mm, 9.973 mm mismatch**, against 002I's 4.464 / 4.464 and
0.000. A mismatch that large is a different board, and it was. The runs were
repeated on the ECO placement; both sets are reported, labelled.

## Three harness defects, found and fixed

- **PR-45** — `connect_hop` picked its via site with `nearest_free()`: *where is
  the closest point a via fits*, when the question is *where is the closest
  point a via fits that this pad can get to*. For `U18.10` it returned a site
  2.30 mm away on the far side of the copper the pin was escaping past. Now the
  near layer is flooded from the escape point and the nearest **reachable**
  via-capable cell is taken — and sites that break `min_hole_to_hole` are
  rejected, which the copper-clearance grids cannot see.
- **PR-46** — two silences. `connect_hop` reported every failure as
  `NO_PATH: no F corridor`, including failures that never reached the far layer,
  so the layer option looked exhausted when it had not been exercised; and a
  connection **rejected by the DRC gate was reverted and requeued with no log
  line at all**. Both now report. That is how
  `BAT_MAIN routed clearance 0.3000 mm; actual 0.2500 mm` became visible.
- **D-249 corridor areas now span both outer layers.** They existed on B.Cu
  only, so an F.Cu escape by a ruled tap fell outside its own corridor and was
  judged against the 0.60 mm class floor. The areas restrict nothing; they only
  name a region for a width rule.

All five validation suites re-run after the changes: **all PASS**.

## What the CTO is being asked

- **D-257** — D-256's instrument does not fit U18's pin field. Options are
  microvia / via-in-pad (a reachable **0.20 mm** site exists 1.20 mm out from
  `U18.10`; the project already declares `min_microvia_diameter 0.20`), a U18
  land-pattern change, moving U18 (forbidden by §7), or accepting `U18.7` and
  `U18.10` unrouted.
- **PR-47** — the Q3 south row is a proved land-pattern conflict: `Q3_CS`
  (pins 1, 3) and `LTC_GATE` (pins 2, 4) interleave with one B.Cu slot, and in
  all three orderings measured the loser's middle pad has no escape at any width
  on either layer.
- **PR-48** — D-249 relaxes **width**, not **clearance**; `U18.1` and the
  U14.2/U14.3 gauge branches are all blocked by `BAT_MAIN routed clearance`.
- **§6 flag** — `LTC_OV` reached F.Cu through the generic fallback (13.604 mm,
  4 vias). It is a high-impedance comparator input and §6 says not to move it
  silently. This is the not-silent part.
