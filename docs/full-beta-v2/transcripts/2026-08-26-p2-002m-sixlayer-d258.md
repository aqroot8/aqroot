## Why it still fails

Two nets, and it is the same trade one region over:

- **`BAT_SENSE Q3.6 → R75.1`** — `BAT_MAIN routed clearance 0.3000 mm; actual
  0.2400 mm`. The 1.00 mm trunk and the POFV copper want the same corridor, and
  a wide net cannot be sent inward: §2 keeps high-current copper on outer 1 oz,
  and the board's own rule says so too. The Kelvin branch itself is fine
  (`R75.1 → U18.9` 3.179 mm) but the net is not one component.
- **`LTC_OV R77.2 → R78.1`** — `NO_VIA_SITE`. §13 forbids adopting the long
  generic F.Cu fallback as the final design, and with the all-layer via check in
  force LTC_OV now finds no legal site at all. **This is §13's stop condition in
  substance.**

Six layers removed the *Q3* conflict, which is what they were bought for. What
is left in the west margin is a clearance contest between a 1.00 mm trunk and a
fine-pitch escape over a few square millimetres — a placement question, and the
U18/R75 levers (§10–§12) were **not** spent, because re-screening U18 against a
prefix that cannot close `BAT_SENSE` or `LTC_OV` would measure the wrong board.
002K is the standing lesson on that.

## Three harness defects, all one family

Adding layers to a router that had only ever seen two:

1. **The obstacle model saw two layers.** The copper set and the routable set
   are now derived from the board, and a through via registers copper on **all**
   of them.
2. **`connect_hop` sited its via checking only `near` and `far`** — harmless
   while those *were* the stack; on six layers it put a via onto another net's
   inner copper and DRC said `shorting_items`. Both hop and POFV siting now
   require the site clear on all six layers.
3. **`connect_hop` could only reach F.Cu**, so the new capacity was unreachable.
   `far` now defaults to every routable layer except `near` — **and never an
   inner layer for a wide net**, which the board's `BAT_MAIN is outer-layer
   only` rule proved three times in one connection.

## The rule correction, a second time

The D-257 escape corridors were emitting a 0.20 mm clearance floor alongside
their via-geometry override. The corridor is grown from the track with a 0.3 mm
tolerance, so it swallows neighbours, and the floor then fired on pairs it never
meant to govern — `LTC_OV` was rejected by a rule named after `LTC_GATE`'s
escape. **A relaxation applied where nothing needed relaxing is a restriction**
— 002L's lesson, repeating. Escape corridors now carry **via geometry only**;
the three measured PR-48 cases keep their clearance where they were measured.
