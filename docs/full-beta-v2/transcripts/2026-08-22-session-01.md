### Claude Code Response — summary

**Two numbers had to be corrected before the gate could mean anything.**

**The programme has been quoting the wrong ERC baseline for three tasks.** FBV2-S1-004, 004B
and 004C all say **"ERC 68"**. The stored reports say **46** — 2 errors, 44 warnings, three
times over. The *deltas* were always right and are reproducible from the files; only the
absolute figure was wrong. Sheet `04`'s migration genuinely took the count from **64 to 46**.

**And there is a flag trap that will catch someone again.** `kicad-cli sch erc --severity-all`
also counts **Exclusions** and reports **104** on the same unmodified design. Ten minutes were
spent chasing a phantom regression before the reports' own *"Report includes: Errors, Warnings"*
header settled it. Every figure in this programme is `--severity-error --severity-warning`.
**Compare like with like or the gate is meaningless.**

### Nothing on Sheet 05 was wrong, and that is the honest headline

The brief said not to copy Beta-DM's BMI270 straps blindly. Every one was re-derived from
**`BST-BMI270-DS000-08` Rev 1.6** — `SDO`→GND for 0x68, `CSB`→VDDIO because Bosch writes
*"it is recommended to hard-wire the CSB line to VDDIO"*, `ASDx`/`ASCx`→VDDIO against Bosch's
explicit ***"Do not connect to GND"***, `INT2`/`OCSB`/`OSDO` left DNC exactly as instructed,
100 nF at pins 5 and 8 — and **they were all already correct**. Saying so plainly is better
than manufacturing a discovery.

### The real defect was on the bus, not the IMU

The internal I²C bus was measured from the netlist rather than assumed: two expanders, the IMU,
the fuel gauge, the TCA9517A A-side, the touch controller through the **50-pin display flex**,
two test points and ~120 mm of trace — **≈ 85 pF worst case**. At **4.7 kΩ** that is
`t_r` = **338 ns, past the 300 ns fast-mode limit**. At a *typical* 60 pF it is 239 ns and
passes.

**That is the worst kind of defect: it works on the bench and fails on the unit with the
longest flex and the widest tolerances**, and the programme's own 100 kHz-then-400 kHz bring-up
rule would have surfaced it late, on hardware, as an intermittent. `R19`/`R20` → **2.2 kΩ**:
158 ns, 47 % margin, **1.32 mA** sink against a 2 mA BMI270, a 6 mA expander and a 3 mA
specification floor. The sink-current check was done *before* the change, as the brief required.

### `0x68` is now a rework instead of a respin

`SDO` was **hard-wired to GND**, so an address collision meant cutting a trace at a 0.25 mm pad.
It is now `R118` 0 Ω **FIT** to GND (0x68) and `R119` 0 Ω **DNP** to `+3V3` (0x69) — *fit one
only*. **`0x68` is the single most collision-prone address on a community I²C bus**: MPU6050,
MPU9250, ICM-20948 and the DS3231/DS1307 RTCs all default to it, and those are exactly what a
hobbyist accessory is built from. **Reserving an address in a document does not stop a $2 module
from arriving at it.**

### GPIO3 became a proof rather than an argument

`INT1_IO_CTRL` **resets to `0x00`** so the output driver is disabled; firmware cannot enable it
before the 8 kB config upload; and ESP32-S3 strap hold time is **`tH` = 3 ms** with GPIO3
defaulting to **Floating**. **The IMU physically cannot reach the strapping window.**

The pull direction then *dictates* the firmware configuration: **push-pull + active-high are
mandatory and open-drain is forbidden**, because an open-drain output into a pull-down never
produces an edge. The GPIO ledger already said this at FBV2-S1-002 from the pull direction
alone; what is new is that the `INT1_IO_CTRL.od` / `.lvl` register definition now backs it, and
it is a recorded contract on the sheet rather than a note. GPIO3 = `RTC_GPIO3`, so EXT0/EXT1
deep-sleep wake works and active-high is the right polarity.

### One capability the brief asked for does not exist

**The BMI270 has no tap or double-tap feature.** The word does not appear in the datasheet. Its
feature set is significant motion / any motion / motion detect / no motion / stationary detect /
wrist wear wakeup / wrist-worn step counter / activity change / push arm down / pivot up / wrist
jiggle / flick in-out. Wake-on-motion, significant motion, orientation, raise-to-wake (that *is*
"wrist wear wakeup") and the 2048-byte FIFO are all supported. **Tap is not, and no hardware is
proposed to compensate.**

### The "DO NOT ROUTE" note on the BMI270 is discharged

§8.3 is a **raster drawing** with nothing in the text layer but *"Pad tolerance: ±50 µm"*. It
was rendered at 12× and the pads measured programmatically, calibrated on the printed 0.5 mm
pitch. **Every printed dimension reproduces** — 0.5, 0.25, 0.475, 0.675, 0.925, 3.0, 2.5 — as
do the pad sizes, the ±1.1625 columns, the ±0.9125 rows and **the peripheral pin order
1–4 left / 5–7 bottom / 8–11 right / 12–14 top**, which is the error that would have been fatal
and silent.

### P-18 — characterised, not decided, and the characterisation moves the problem

**`U16` TCA9517A, `R49`/`R50`, `U15` and `D2`/`D3` are ALL DNP.** There is no fitted external
I²C path today, so the Sheet 09 choice costs **no rework**. TI settles the powered-off case:
*"the TCA9517A logic and all I/Os are powered by the `VCCB` pin"*, and `VCCB` is `ACC_3V3_SW`,
so a de-asserted rail leaves the buffer unpowered and high-Z on both sides — **harder than a
mux, which stays powered**.

**The weakness is not the buffer. It is the location of its disable control.** `ACC_PWR_EN` is
`U3` P17 — an expander output sitting behind the very bus a broken accessory would hold low. A
9-clock recovery pulse train frees the common case for nothing; a hard short escapes only
through a `+3V3` power cycle, because an MCU reset does not reset the expanders.

**O-4 is raised for CTO decision** — a TCA4307-class hot-swap buffer with stuck-bus recovery at
Sheet 09 migration, because the community header is a hot-plug connector by definition and that
part is the only option that both pre-charges on insertion and recovers without the host. It is
**not pin-compatible**, and its MPN must come from a live listing (D-096). **Nothing was
implemented.**

### What was NOT done

No new buffer, no I²C mux, no load switch (it would save ≈ 9 µA and destroy wake-on-motion), no
second interrupt pin, no alternate IMU footprint, no supply test point, no `RESERVED_SPARE`
consumed. **One inherited discrepancy is recorded and deliberately left alone:** `U2`/`U3` still
carry the value `TCA9535PWR` while D-061 locked NXP `PCAL9535APW,118` — the address base is
identical and both parts live on **sheet 08**, which is not authorised here. Sheets `06`-`09`,
the PCB, mechanical CAD, firmware, Beta-DM and frozen Beta untouched. Sheet `06` not begun.
