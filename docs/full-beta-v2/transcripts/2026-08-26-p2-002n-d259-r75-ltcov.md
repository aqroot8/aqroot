## The R75 measurement that matters

**Translating R75 was never going to fix the Kelvin mismatch.** `U18.8` and
`U18.9` are at the **same y**, and R75 is a **5.925 mm** shunt whose pads lie
along y — so any north/south move changes both Kelvin lengths equally and the
mismatch stays at very nearly the shunt's own length. Over a ±8 mm box at
0.5 mm in four rotations, **28 poses reached the Kelvin test, all rot 90/270,
best mismatch 5.177 mm against a 5.000 mm limit.**

Turning the shunt so its pads lie along x drops the mismatch below 1 mm — and
**every rot 0/180 pose is blocked by the R80/R81/R82 courtyards at x 7.30.**
The part costing the Kelvin spec is the divider column, not R75.

§6 does not pin that column, so it was offered the smallest eastward shift that
frees the rotation. **1.0 mm** is the minimum, and it yields 8 poses; the best is
**R75 (4.300, 63.500) rot 180 — Kelvin 7.772 / 7.000, mismatch 0.771 mm.**

**And the combination does not hold.** Screened on the six-layer prefix, moving
the column east puts BAT_MAIN copper into the LTC4368 control lanes:

```
LTC_SHDN U18.6 -> R80.2   BAT_MAIN routed clearance 0.3000; actual 0.2750
LTC_GATE U18.10-> Q3.4    ...                                     0.2778
FAULT_N  R82.1 -> Q9.1    ...                                     0.2371
```

U18 fell to **6/8**. The Kelvin fix is real; the price is paid one region over.

## A harness change made, measured, and reverted

`BAT_MAIN routed clearance` fires on either side of the pair, so a control track
passing a BAT_MAIN **pad** looks like it owes 0.300 mm — and `margin()` returned
0.200 mm for every pad regardless of net, which is why DRC kept reporting
actuals between the two figures. Raising wide-net pads to 0.300 mm looked like
the fix.

**It is not.** With the bump in force, `U18.8` and `U18.9` — the D-249-ruled
Kelvin taps that must leave an MSOP-10 field past R75's own pads — both came back
`NO_LEGAL_ESCAPE`. They route legally today at **0.150 mm** under the pad-escape
necking block, a later and more specific rule, so DRC does not demand 0.300 mm
there. A router-side margin cannot see which corridor rule governs a pair, so it
would have to over-apply — and **over-applying a clearance is how a legal escape
becomes NO_LEGAL_ESCAPE.** Reverted, with the reasoning left in the code.
